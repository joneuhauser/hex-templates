Hexahedral meshes of Schneiders’ pyramid

We enumerate cell connectivities for a pyramid with a prescribed quadrilateral boundary, then optimize the interior vertex coordinates. The boundary has four quadrilaterals on its base and three on each triangular side.

The collection shows 55 connectivities found by the search, with 36–48 cells, plus the published 88-cell mesh. Each exported cell has a strictly positive Jacobian determinant throughout its reference cube.

The two-mirror enumeration covers both axial and diagonal orientations through 48 cells. Counts below exclude symmetric copies and candidates without a validated embedding.

Improved element quality

H44-class4 achieves a minimum sampled scaled Jacobian of 0.359, higher than every literature-reference connectivity displayed here: 0.316 for the optimized 36-cell reference, 0.144 for the published 44-cell mesh, and 0.160 for the 88-cell mesh. It offers a quality improvement at 44 cells.

Search setup and references

What is searched
Conforming hexahedral fillings of Schneiders’ pyramid. The entire 18-vertex, 16-quad boundary is prescribed: four base quads and three on each triangular face. Boundary coordinates stay fixed during HexOpt embedding.
Starting point
The search starts with the boundary and no interior cells. Interior vertices and cells are introduced by the solver; no literature connectivity is used as a seed.
Symmetry
Two perpendicular vertical mirrors are required: either the axial pair x = 0, y = 0 or the diagonal pair x = y, x = −y. The enumeration covers both choices through 48 cells. Symmetric copies are identified under the pyramid’s boundary symmetries.
Search limits
The default run-pyramid.sh uses the diagonal pair and a cap of 36 cells, with no time limit. Set THREADS to the worker count. The gallery includes certified embeddings through 48 cells and literature meshes; these constrained searches do not establish an unrestricted minimum.
Literature
Published solutions include 36 cells by Xiang and Liu, 44 by Verhetsel, Pellerin and Remacle, and 88 by Yamakawa and Shimada. The search finds both the published 36-cell and 44-cell connectivities. The 36-cell mesh uses coordinates optimized here; the 44-cell mesh (H44-class16) retains the authors’ published coordinates. The 88-cell mesh is included for comparison and lies beyond the 48-cell search cap.
